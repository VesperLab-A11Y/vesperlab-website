Vesper Tools

The tools I build for my own audits, published in open access. Free, with no account needed, and held to the same accessibility standards as what I audit.

  • Published

    Vesper Toolkit

    A dozen accessibility-testing tools in your bookmarks bar. No extension, no sign-up, no data that leaves the browser.

    What it's for
    Running automated scans of a page to get a first read: axe-core, contrast, tab order, accessible name, heading hierarchy, form labels, and several other targeted tools.
    How to use it
    Drag the bookmarklets from the install page (toolkit.vesperlab.dev), then click one on the page you want to test.
    What it doesn't do
    Doesn't replace a manual check: axe-core only catches part of the WCAG criteria, the rest is still down to human judgment.
    Technology and license
    Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, no dependencies, MIT licensed.

    Currently available in English only; a French translation is in progress.

    Vesper Library

    A library of accessible interface components: a demo to try with keyboard and screen reader, a code snippet, and a WCAG checklist for each one.

    What it's for
    29 common interface components (menus, forms, dialogs, tabs…), each tested with a screen reader, with reference code and the WCAG 2.2 criteria to check point by point.
    How to use it
    Search for a component, copy the code snippet, or turn on Report Mode to check off verified points during a diagnostic and export your annex. Directly from (library.vesperlab.dev)
    What it doesn't do
    Doesn't cover components that are very brand-specific: the 29 entries stay generic patterns, to be adapted to context.
    Technology and license
    Static HTML/CSS/JS page, no dependencies, self-hosted fonts, MIT licensed.

    Bilingual French/English: the language toggle translates all the content, not just the interface.

    Vesper Compass and Sonar

    An educational, filterable reference of the WCAG 2.2 criteria, to better grasp digital accessibility and understand the real needs behind the code.

    What it's for
    Keeping the digital accessibility criteria close at hand, understanding them, pulling them up whenever needed, and putting real human needs behind the rules.
    How to use it
    Install the Sonar bookmarklet to display Compass in a window, right on the page you're visiting. Visit (compass.vesperlab.dev) for the full glossary, with a search bar.
    What it doesn't do
    Replace the official texts! Compass exists to make the regulation approachable and human, not to serve as an authority in an accessibility remediation process.
    Technology and license
    Static HTML/CSS/JS page, no dependencies, self-hosted fonts, MIT licensed. The criteria come from the same dataset as Toolkit, generated once for both.

    Vesper AC Viewer

    The best companion to the Toolkit bookmarklets, for viewing and exporting your scan results.

    What it's for
    Viewing the results of the 🦇 AC Scan and 🦇 AC Targeted Scan bookmarklets in an accessible way, showing details, and exporting the data in several formats.
    How to use it
    Visit (the Viewer page) and paste your results. The bookmarklets are available on (toolkit.vesperlab.dev)
    What it doesn't do
    Doesn't run a scan itself: you first need a JSON produced by AC Scan (or one of Toolkit's other scans that exports in the same format) to paste in. The report stays axe-core's raw output: to sort through and interpret like any automated scan.
    Technology and license
    Standalone HTML/JS page, but it loads two external libraries from a CDN to generate the exports: ExcelJS (.xlsx) and docx.js (.docx). MIT licensed.

    Vesper Visible

    One and the same site, two pages: one designed to be accessible from the start, the other where accessibility was improvised. See — and hear — the differences for yourself, if you can find them.

    What it's for
    Understanding accessibility concretely through a direct comparison, trying out the (Vesper Toolkit) bookmarklets, watching automated scans in action and understanding their limits, opening up the field of the visible.
    How to use it
    Visit (Vesper Visible) and run your own experiments: you can use the bookmarklets from the (Vesper Toolkit), color tools, a screen reader, and your browser's dev tools. To dig even deeper, several accessibility barriers stay invisible to any scan.
    What it doesn't do
    The site itself serves no real function: it doesn't represent a business and has no real pages or content to offer. It also won't hand you (all) the answers directly: they're scattered through the scan results, the code, and various toggles, but no link will compare the elements side by side for you.
    Technology and license
    Three static HTML/CSS/JS pages, no dependencies, self-hosted fonts, MIT licensed.
